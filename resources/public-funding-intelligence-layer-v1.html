The Public Funding Layer
Capstone concept · Discovery brief
Today Tomorrow Moat Tracks Risks
UCLA–NUS GEMBA Intake 22 · Capstone concept · Pre-vertical, pre-product

Public capital is available. Reaching it is the broken part.

Governments make enormous amounts of capital available every year for housing, healthcare, research, infrastructure, manufacturing and media. Organizations spend that same year hunting for exactly that capital. Between them sits no shared data structure — only fragmented programs, prose eligibility rules, bespoke applications and institutional memory. We are not proposing a better grant finder. We are proposing the intelligence and coordination layer for the ecosystem.

01
Current state

Two processes that never touch

An organization seeking capital searches, interprets eligibility from long prose notices, assembles a stack from grants, credits, debt and equity, then files bespoke applications program by program. Large teams absorb this cost. Smaller ones cannot.

An agency designs a program from last cycle's assumptions, publishes a notice, waits, and reviews whatever pool arrives. When the pool is thin or unqualified, it has no way to learn why.

The friction is not in any single step. It is that both columns run top to bottom independently and nothing crosses. The consultant class exists precisely because that gap is unstructured and unpriced.

02
Future state

One spine that both sides write to

Three components carry the weight. An eligibility graph that turns prose requirements into rules a project profile can be tested against. A stack composer that knows which sources of public and private capital can legally and practically combine. A signal ledger that records what applicants attempted, where they dropped, what got deployed and what it produced.

Neither side gives anything up. Both get something they cannot obtain alone.

The product is not the interface. The product is the structured record of what happened, which is exactly what neither a generic AI assistant nor an incumbent consultant can manufacture.
Why it compounds

The loop is the business. Everything else is a feature.

Better information for applicants→ More qualified projects→ Better information for agencies→ Better program design→ Better outcomes

Information generated on one side of the ecosystem makes the product more valuable to the other. That is a data and network advantage, and it is the only part of this concept that a well-funded fast follower cannot buy or prompt its way into.

03
Differentiation

Three assets are defensible. The rest is table stakes.

The moat is not AI. Generic tools will search documents, summarise regulation and help draft applications — and they will do it well enough, soon enough, to erase any product anchored there.
Asset 01

Eligibility as a computed graph

Not retrieval over program PDFs — machine-evaluable rules a specific project profile can be tested against, across jurisdictions and statutes that cross-reference each other.

Why it holdsThis is a data engineering problem wearing an AI costume. It requires structuring prose that is revised mid-cycle. Generic tools will not do this work.
Asset 02

Stacking combinatorics

Finding one program is a search feature. Knowing that a federal credit disqualifies a state grant, that two subsidies layer only in sequence, and that a lender waits on a specific close — that is the expertise being sold today.

Why it holdsRules plus precedent. The precedent half only accumulates by sitting in the flow of real transactions.
Asset 03

The demand signal

Agencies cannot see their own funnel: who considered a program, where they abandoned it, and why. This is the only view that makes an agency a paying customer rather than a data source.

Why it holdsIt can only be produced by standing between both sides. There is no dataset to license and no model to prompt.
Commoditised within 18 months — do not anchor the MVP here
  • Plain-language explanation of program requirements
  • Semantic search across funding notices
  • Deadline tracking and reminder workflows
  • First-draft application and narrative generation

If the findings diverge

Vertical-specific solution

Housing, healthcare, manufacturing and media turn out to have genuinely different problems. Useful information — it pushes us toward depth in one market rather than a horizontal platform, and it narrows the MVP fast.

If the findings converge

Horizontal infrastructure

Fragmented programs, difficult eligibility analysis and incomplete information recur across all four verticals — while agency interviews reveal the mirror image. That is a far more interesting result and the one worth building for.

05
Known tradeoffs

Three things the concept has to survive

Cold start is asymmetric
The applicant side has value on day one — better search beats no search. The agency side has value only once applicant volume exists. That sequencing is fixed, which means early revenue will look like a SaaS tool, not infrastructure. The risk is over-serving that first customer and never building the ledger.
Agencies buy slowly
Public procurement runs on 9–18 month cycles, RFPs and reference checks. If the capstone demo is agency-facing, it has to be sellable to a program manager on discretionary budget — not to a CIO through a formal procurement.
The eligibility graph decays
Rules change every cycle, so maintenance cost scales with program coverage rather than with customers. That is the opposite of software economics — and it is precisely why the asset stays defensible. It also means "cover every program" is a losing opening move. Depth in one jurisdiction beats breadth.

Proposed first step

Structured customer discovery. No commitment yet.

Each member conducts a small number of conversations in their track and brings findings back to the group — compared rather than assumed identical. Six questions decide what happens next.
01

Is there a real and sufficiently painful problem?

02

Where is the pain greatest, and does it recur across industries?

03

Who would actually pay to solve it — applicant, agency, or the capital provider between them?

04

Where could we obtain differentiated data that others cannot?

05

Which vertical offers the strongest initial wedge?

06

What is the smallest product buildable during the capstone that demonstrates genuine value?

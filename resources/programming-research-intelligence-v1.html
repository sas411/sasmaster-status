01The findings that survived

Six claims, each verified against live data and stress-tested for the obvious rebuttal. Where a headline changed under scrutiny, the changed version is what appears here.

1 · The catalogue score is a format detector, not an audience signal

Public catalogue popularity is close to uninformative against measured audience (rho +0.128, n=243), and review score is near-zero (+0.034). The apparent "low-rated content delivers more" gradient is genre composition, not quality:

The catalogue rates scripted content and barely rates news and talk — the formats carrying the most linear viewing. So a low score on a linear schedule mostly indicates format. Ranking a schedule by catalogue score systematically misprices what actually delivers.

Do not state it this way

"Best-reviewed titles deliver 3.5× less audience" is false at the typical title — the median is non-monotone and Q4's median is above Q1's. The pooled gradient is real but composition-driven. Claim the mechanism, never the inversion.

2 · Catalogue depth out-predicts every metric the industry cites

Episode count correlates rho +0.355 with audience per telecast (n=140) — roughly 2.8× the correlation of catalogue popularity on the same universe. Titles with 300+ episodes deliver a median 1.464 versus 0.180 for those under 50. Deep libraries are strip-scheduling inventory; a prestige 30-episode run is not. This is a free, near-universally-populated field outperforming the two metrics the market actually prices on.

3 · Library economics — older catalogue out-delivers newer

Scripted titles originating pre-1990 deliver 7.96× the median audience per telecast of scripted 2010+ (0.947 vs 0.119, n=20 vs 58). The scripted/unscripted split is deliberate — it kills the "you're just measuring news and sports" objection, and the finding survives it.

Bounded by calendar, not by sample

The window is late May to early June — peak rerun season, which structurally favours library. More titles inside this window will not resolve it; only more weeks will. Frame as "in the summer window" or do not frame.

4 · Viewing is stockpiled, not distributed

The heaviest viewing quintile supplies 82.65% of all weighted viewing minutes (n=115,136 persons, 12 complete weeks). No cable network is broadly consumed: the top 10% of households supply between 59.7% (the flattest network measured) and 98.9% of a network's minutes. Quintiles were derived in-house — the reference table for them is empty, and the derivation method is stated in the service payload.

5 · Time-shifting is a broadcast behaviour, and it is over in six hours

59.18% of time-shifted minutes replay within 6 hours, 76% within a day, and only 7.91% across days 3–7. Broadcast shifts at 19.54% versus cable at 10.19% — a 1.92× difference. This sizes the entire commercial-window argument: the tail everyone negotiates over is under 8% of shifted viewing. Derived from delay fields directly, never from differencing the viewing lanes.

6 · Supply reallocated into the worst-surviving genres

Comedy fell from 23.88% to 16.73% of new series (2010→2025) while drama rose 16.83% → 34.47%. Second-season conversion, on a fixed 3-year window, runs Comedy 31.2% (n=7,310), Drama 21.6% (n=10,194), Romance 15.5% (n=2,747). The industry moved supply toward the genres least likely to return — and romance is simultaneously the fastest-growing share and the worst renewer in the catalogue.

Do not state it this way

"Comedy and drama crossed in 2021" is a full-catalogue artifact. In the externally-matched cohort, drama already led before 2015. The direction is robust in both cohorts; the crossing date is not a fact about the industry.

02Claims that are dead, and why

These are not cautious omissions. Each is a specific measurement that the data cannot support, verified. Anything reproducing them is wrong regardless of how reasonable it looks.

ClaimStatusWhy it is dead
Delayed-window lift (L+3 / L+7 uplift)DEAD The viewing lanes do not reconcile. The delayed lane reads below the as-aired lane on 19.7% of title×demo cells and 7,701 of 24,494 telecasts, with backwards mass ≈ forward mass. Confirmed independently at two grains.
Commercial-minute audience (C3 / C7)DEAD Those fields are byte-identical to the programme average across all 4.25M measured rows — max difference exactly 0.0. The column names exist; the signal does not.
Any audience-age or demographic claimDEAD The two audience surfaces carry demographic coverage running in opposite directions, and neither is documented as authoritative. The answer depends on which table you query.
Absolute viewer counts as a headlineDEAD The unit is uncertified and the two surfaces disagree by roughly two orders of magnitude. Ratios, medians, shares and ranks only.
Goodness-of-fit statistics (R²)DEAD At these sample sizes it flatters a finding rather than testing it, and implies a model never estimated. A prior rank-based R²=1.00 was a false positive by construction.
National television framingDEAD The serving rollup carries no broadcast audience at all; the measured universe there is cable-heavy. Broadcast is measured at base-table grain — the gap is a rollup, not the warehouse.

Fifteen missing descriptor partitions stand between a 370-title anecdote and a defensible product claim. Where descriptors exist, identity matching lands at 98.9%; where they do not, it is structurally zero. Back-filling them is expected to take descriptor coverage from roughly a third to near-complete. The lead finding's obvious rebuttal — "you are just measuring that cable skews old and the catalogue skews young" — dies only by stratifying within genre, daypart and network, and every such cut is currently under n=20. 06Method notes worth keeping

  • Telecast counts repeat across demographic buckets. Summing them inflates any per-telecast denominator ~30×. De-duplicate before dividing.
  • Telecast keys are reused within a year. Joining identity without the air date attaches wrong titles to real audience — invisible in aggregate. 1,560 measured keys matched only on a different date.
  • Aggregate demographics are not queryable — the serving layer carries atomic buckets only. Aggregates must be derived by summing disjoint buckets, valid for audience counts and never for rates or shares.
  • Zero-scored titles are unrated, not bad. Excluding them strengthened the score finding rather than weakening it — which is how we knew it was composition, not contamination.
  • A hypothesis that dies is a result. Three of this pass's most valuable outputs were killed claims.

Proving Ground · the forward-looking instrument

A market for stories that don’t exist yet.

Proving Ground creates demand for scripts that have not been made. It renders unproduced scripts as 30-second trailers, puts them in front of fans, and measures what those fans do — not what they say. That demand is priced, sold to the people who fund production, and used for one purpose: getting the scripts fans want made.

Supply finds demand — an unmade script is read by the audience it was written for. Demand finds supply — what that audience does is priced and handed to the people who can make it. The demand data is the product; the produced script is the point.

01 The one-line versionLoaded, defaults (E)

Proving Ground creates the demand, sells the data, and gets the script made.

It renders unmade scripts and concepts as watchable 30-second trailers, puts them in front of a live global audience, and converts what that audience watches, finishes, replays and ranks head-to-head into a priced demand signal — resolved against the certified identity spine.

Three things follow, in order. Demand is created where none could exist before — fans discover a script through its trailer. The demand data is sold — to studios, streamers and buyers as certificates, panels and a standing feed. The script gets produced — the buyer who paid for the signal is the buyer who options it. Move the audience test from rough cut to first draft, drop the cost of asking by three orders of magnitude, and sell the answer to the people writing the cheques.

03 The loop behind the readInstrumented

Render → Vote → Resolve → Price. A certificate at the end.

Four stages, each instrumented. A script is rendered as a 30-second trailer; a live audience votes with behaviour — completion, replay, scroll-away, head-to-head; the concept is resolved against the identity spine; the result is priced and signed.

The loop runs at first draft, in days, for cents a read. It does not end in a report: a priced concept leaves the loop toward a buyer, and the buyer's option is what turns demand into a produced title. The certificate is the product; production is the point.

08 Three sidesDraft terms

Writers get produced, paid and acknowledged; studios get a price; audiences get a say.

Mara gives one script, non-exclusive, revocable. Theo gives slate titles under NDA and 30 minutes a month. Dani gives attention. Each leaves with something priced before the money.

Each side gives something the others cannot supply, and gets a price before the money is spent. Three sides, one clearing, both directions.

Who is the customer? Studios, streamers and buyers pay — for closed panels, signed certificates and the Demand Feed. Writers pay nothing; they get produced. Consumers pay with attention (and, at P1, a subscription) for a say in what gets made. The demand data is what is sold; the produced script is what everyone came for.

Will a writer let the world see the script? No — and they never have to. The script is never shown. The trailer is public, the script stays private, the Demand Index is private by default, and the rights partner co-designs the terms. Incentive, not exposure, is what brings the premium scripts in.

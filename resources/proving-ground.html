01

The Thesis

First principles

Strip the industry to first principles and it has exactly two unsolved problems, and they are the same problem. Demand: every signal the business owns is a post-mortem — box office, Nielsen delivery, streaming minutes all measure things already written, already cast, already paid for. The one forward-looking instrument, the test screening, arrives at rough cut: $10–20K, 300–500 recruits, and 100% of the negative already spent. Supply: the best-liked scripts in Hollywood sit unmade, because nobody can price the audience for a thing that doesn't exist.

Proving Ground prices it. It renders unmade scripts and concepts as watchable 30-second trailers, puts them in front of a live global audience, and converts what that audience watches, finishes, replays and ranks head-to-head into a priced demand signal — resolved against the same certified identity spine that already ties viewership, metadata and financial ground truth into one comparable frame.

The one-line version

Move the audience test from rough cut to first draft, drop the cost of asking by three orders of magnitude, and sell the answer to the people writing the cheques.

The Stack — Different Models, Different Jobs

Best-in-role · every seat swappable

No single model can run this loop well, so none is asked to. Each seat goes to the model that currently wins it — and every seat sits behind the Model Gateway, a provenance wall where a model earns production status through a bake-off and loses it the same way. The models are components. The spine, the telemetry and the certificates are the IP.

Seat 01 · Orchestrator & Creative Director

CLAUDE

Anthropic

Runs the agent fleet end to end: rights triage on intake, prompt direction per shot, and the QC gate that holds every render to one fixed house standard — so the test measures the story, not the generator. Also the surface the platform itself is built and shipped on.

Swap cost: highest — orchestration is the deepest integration
Seat 02 · Script Decomposition

GEMINI

Google

Reads a full 110-page screenplay in one pass and returns structure: beat sheet, the three most trailer-able moments, shot plan, tone and casting references. Long-context comprehension is the whole job description for this seat.

Swap cost: low — structured output, re-run the bake-off
Seat 03 · Trailer Generation

SEEDANCE

ByteDance

Turns the shot plan into the 30-second trailer: multi-shot text-and-image-to-video with character consistency across cuts, at roughly $2 a take. The fastest-moving seat in the stack — which is exactly why it is a seat and not a foundation.

Swap cost: lowest — renderers leapfrog quarterly by design
Seat 04 · Neural Pre-Screen

TRIBE

Meta FAIR

A trimodal brain encoder that predicts human neural response to video — winner of the Algonauts 2025 challenge. Ranks candidate renders by predicted engagement before real audience attention is spent. Experimental tier: a calibration layer, never a verdict.

Swap cost: n/a — advisory only, gated from the certificate
The posture

Named today, married never. Every external model enters through the Gateway with a scored bake-off against the incumbent and a walk-away threshold. If a better decomposer, renderer or encoder ships next quarter, the seat turns over in weeks — and the demand data, the identity spine and every certificate already issued do not move an inch.

Revenue — Four Engines, Sequenced

Order is the strategy

Four engines is the destination, not the starting line. Run in parallel they compete for the same scarce thing — attention and capital. Run in this order, each one pays for the next.

The sequencing logic

Panels fund the feed. The feed creates the data. The data creates the licensing business. The licensing business creates the option flow. Reverse that order and you are running a consumer media company with no revenue and no comparables — which is the failure mode of every script-voting platform that came before.

09

Supply — Three Waves

Rights risk rises with signal quality
Wave 1 · Calibrate

Public domain & lapsed rights

Zero clearance risk, instant catalog, and the honest way to calibrate the instrument before a single writer puts their IP at stake. Weak novelty signal — used to tune, not to sell.

Wave 1 · Volume

User-submitted loglines

Open intake. Infinite supply, near-zero acquisition cost, and the engine of the consumer feed. Noisy, needs originality screening and moderation. This is where virality comes from.

Wave 2 · Quality

Opted-in spec scripts

The strongest public signal — real screenplays that never got made. Requires explicit writer license, revenue share, and unambiguous terms on training use. The option flow is what buys this material.

Wave 3 · Revenue

Studio slates under NDA

Confidential by design: no public voting, closed panels, results visible only to the client. This is the supply side of the panel business and it never touches the consumer feed.

10

The Validation Gate

Before anyone prices a deal on this

The instrument is not credible until it has been back-tested against reality, in public, with the result published either way.

The test

Take 200 already-released titles with known realized performance in the spine. Render their original loglines as concept trailers. Run them through the panel blind — no titles, no cast, no marketing. Correlate the resulting demand index against realized box office, delivery and licensing revenue.

If it holds, the instrument is real and the number has a price. If it does not, that gets published too. The precedent is set: the sibling script-ratings pilot scored nearly 10,000 titles, found that summary text explains under 2% of variance, and said so rather than burying it. Same discipline applies here.

11

What Would Break It

Stated up front, not discovered later
Risk 01 · Construct validity

Premise is not picture

A 30-second trailer measures appetite for a premise, not for a finished film. The gap between the two is the central empirical question, and Section 10 exists to size it before anyone trades on it.

Risk 02 · Confound

Execution contaminates signal

A weak render kills a strong script. Unless render quality is held to a fixed standard across the panel, the instrument measures the generator instead of the story. This is why the orchestrator seat owns a hard QC gate — controlled by design, not by hope.

Risk 03 · Rights & consent

Guild and writer posture

Generating video from a writer's script requires explicit, revocable licence and a stated position on training use. Opt-in only, revenue-shared, unambiguous. Getting this wrong is not a legal problem — it is a supply problem, permanently.

Risk 04 · Adversarial

Any public vote is brigadeable

Stated preference can be bought. Completion, replay, scroll-away point and forced head-to-head choice are far harder to manufacture at scale — which is why the revealed-preference layer, not the thumb, is what gets sold.

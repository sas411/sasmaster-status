StoryForge opens its horror feed: every script gets a trailer, every greenlight gets a number

Writers see their unproduced scripts as 30-second concept trailers. Fans pick between them four times a night. Studios read a Demand Certificate before they spend.

LOS ANGELES and LONDON. StoryForge today opened its first public feed, a slate of horror concepts that do not exist yet as films. Each begins as a writer's own script, is rendered as a short concept trailer the writer approves, and is shown head-to-head to a paid panel in the US and UK. Four hundred paired reads later, the concept carries a Demand Index with an error bar.

Until now the first audience read on a film arrived at rough cut, from a recruited screening costing about $25,000, after most of the budget was committed. A StoryForge read costs under $200 and arrives at first draft.

"Reading a script predicts almost nothing. Watching might. We built the instrument that finds out, and we publish the result either way."Shiv Sehgal, founder

Writers keep 100% of their rights and no script is used to train a model. Trailers are test stimuli and are never released as films. Studios read private slates under NDA; the public feed is opt-in.

Frequently asked · customers
Does trailer reaction actually predict outcomes?

We don't know yet, and we say so. Gate zero is one blind, pre-registered back-test against films whose results are already public. It costs about $18K and is published whether it passes or fails.

I'm a writer. What happens to my script?

It stays yours. Private by default, no training on your material, 100% of rights kept. You approve the beats before anything renders, and you leave with a sell kit: one-sheet, treatment and concept trailer.

I'm a fan. What do I get for voting?

You see which one won straight after the tap, and what happened next to every concept. If your pick gets made you get early access and a thanks credit. Rewards are earned, never bought.

Is this AI slop?

The story comes from a writer, the beats are approved, the library is curated and there is no infinite feed. The bar is premium TV, not the scroll. Ten of sixteen interviewees said AI is fine if the quality is there.

Frequently asked · internal
What stops a studio building this itself?

A studio can build a renderer. It cannot build a calibration ledger of reads joined to realised outcomes across many buyers. The renderer is replaceable; the ledger is the moat.

What does a buyer pay?

Open. No buyer has quoted a number yet. Five pricing conversations are part of gate zero, and the list price is a hypothesis until then.

Does it survive model costs going up?

The logged render cost on the trailers above is $1.20 per 8-second shot. The stress test still to pass is surviving a 100× rise in token cost before scale.

Who is on the panel?

Paid panellists in the US and UK, 18 to 34 for the horror wedge, one person one tap. Released films are seeded in as known answers, and panellists who read those right weigh more.
